# Heavy Bag — Weight-sourcing research: how to avoid shipping 24 kg of metal per customer

_Research note, 2026-10-29. Status: exploratory, not a decision. Unless a source is cited, numbers are **estimates (E)**. Prices are rough listing prices from Oct 2026 searches._

## 0. Framing: the numbers that drive everything

**Volume needed for 24 kg** (solid volume, then a realistic packed volume including a container or shell, ~10–25% overhead):

| Source | Density g/cm³ | 24 kg volume | Briefcase base strip 38 × 10 cm → height | Backpack sleeve 28 × 40 cm → thickness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solid steel / cast iron (coated) | 7.9 / 7.2 | 3.0–3.3 L | ~8–9 cm | ~2.7–3 cm |
| Steel shot, loose-poured bulk | **~4.4** (4.4–4.8 shaken) [1] | 5.5 L | ~14 cm | ~4.9 cm |
| Magnetite ("heavyweight") concrete | 3.5–4.0 [2] | 6–7 L | ~16–18 cm | ~5.5–6 cm |
| Normal concrete / clay brick | 1.8–2.4 | 10–13 L | 27–35 cm ✗ | 9–12 cm ✗ |
| Sand (sealed pouch) | ~1.6 | 15 L | ✗ | 13 cm ✗ |
| Water | 1.0 | 24 L | ✗ | ✗ |
| Paper (reams/books) | ~0.8–1.2 | 20–30 L | ✗ | ✗ |

**Key insight 1. Only metal or metal-loaded fillers fit the full 24 kg in a slim bag.** Anything at ≤2.4 g/cm³ (concrete, bricks, sand, water) tops out at about **8–12 kg** in a compartment of acceptable size. That's still useful: it is the beginner range, and most users will spend their first weeks or months there.

**Key insight 2. Shipping metal costs less than it feels like it should.** Ocean freight to a regional warehouse costs cents per kg. The expensive leg is the last-mile parcel. Data points:
- Rogue Fitness, US UPS Ground: roughly "weight ÷ 2 in dollars per lb", i.e. ~$1.1/kg, so 24 kg ≈ **$25–30** at a large shipper's negotiated rates [3].
- EU dealer rates for parcels up to 30 kg: DHL €14.90, UPS Express Saver €24.50 [4]. Older flat-rate EU example: $35 for 0–25 kg [4].
- Retail or small-shipper rates are 2–3× higher. **E:** US $50–90, cross-border EU €30–60.
- Bag (~3 kg + box) plus 24 kg of weights crosses the common 25 kg parcel cap → **ship the weights as separate parcels of ≤ 12–15 kg** (two or three boxes).

So "ship our own bricks" costs roughly **$25–60 per full 24 kg set** at scale (E), plus about $2–4/kg to make coated cast iron (E). The full set lands at roughly **$75–150**. That's not prohibitive for a premium bag, but it is heavy friction for an entry-price impulse purchase, for returns, and for international markets. The best answer is to **decouple the bag from the weight source**, and to ship metal only in small increments, when the customer actually needs it.

**Key insight 3. Users progress.** A starter carries maybe 4–8 kg of added weight. Nobody needs 24 kg on day one. That makes "ship a little steel now, more later", or "start with local filler, upgrade to dense", natural.

## 1. Idea catalogue (24 ideas)

Scores (1–5, higher is better): **F** = feasibility (engineering and ops), **A** = customer appeal, **C** = cost to us and customer (5 = cheapest).

### A. DIY casting and filling

**1. Mould kit: cast your own concrete bricks**
- **What:** Ship 2–4 reusable HDPE/PP or silicone moulds matching the tray slot (e.g. 38 × 10 × 3 cm). The customer casts bricks from bagged concrete mix and seals them with paint, a plastic sleeve or a dip coating.
- **Requirements:** Adjustable at ~2.5 kg per brick at density 2.3 (38 × 10 × 3 cm ≈ 1.1 L ≈ 2.6 kg). Quick drop works with the tray. Replaceable: yes, cast more. Rattle-free only with a sleeve and clamp. **Compactness fails above ~10 kg**: 24 kg needs ~10 L.
- **Shipping:** Moulds are light, under 1 kg.
- **Unit cost / model:** Moulds cost $1–5 to make (paver moulds retail $10–25 in the US, ₹25–60 wholesale in India [5]). Sell for $20–30.
- **Customer effort:** High: mixing, a 1–2 day cure, sealing.
- **Risks:** Dust and abrasion on the lining, edges chipping, inconsistent weight. Wet-cement skin burns. Crumbling bricks rattle.
- **Scores:** F4 A2 C5.
- **Variant 1b, magnetite or steel-shot concrete:** Adding iron-ore or steel-shot aggregate raises density to 3.5–4+ [2], which makes 24 kg in ~6.5 L possible. Industrial magnetite is hard to buy retail. Steel shot is easier (blast media at $1–4/kg). Scores: F3 A2 C4.

**2. Fill-your-own sealed cassettes (rigid shells shipped empty)**
- **What:** Blow-moulded or rotomoulded HDPE cassettes (e.g. 38 × 10 × 3.5 cm, ~1.2 L) with a screw cap. They nest or flat-stack for shipping. The customer fills them with steel shot, sand or gravel, then caps them.
- **Requirements:** Every cassette has the same outer shape, so every one fits every slot (R-K1). The weight depends on the filler: about **5.3 kg with steel shot, ~2 kg with sand, ~1.2 kg with water**. Filled solid, there is no internal rattle. A slightly-under-filled cassette needs a foam spacer.
- **Shipping:** ~150–250 g each (E). Ten cassettes ≈ 2 kg, shipped with the bag.
- **Unit cost:** $1.50–4 each at volume (E). Blow-mould tooling ~$5–20k (E).
- **Customer effort:** Medium: buy filler, use a funnel, weigh with a scale.
- **Risks:** Cap leak (sand or shot spilling in the bag); use a bonded cap or a one-time snap seal. Weights aren't marked, so ship labels and a scale.
- **Scores:** F5 A4 C5. **One of the strongest ideas.**

**3. Fill pouches (soft, sewn TPU-coated fabric) for shot or sand**
- **What:** Like GORUCK sandbag "filler" bags, which ship empty for local filling [6]. Pouch sizes match tray slots, with a roll-top or welded valve and a double seal.
- **Requirements:** Conform to the tray, so no rattle when compressed. Adjustable by fill amount. Shot gives ~4.4 g/cm³. Pouches are less precise than rigid cassettes, and they slump.
- **Shipping and cost:** Near zero shipping. Pouches cost $2–5 each (E).
- **Customer effort:** Medium.
- **Risks:** Abrasion leaks over ~10,000 lifts; plan a double-wall or inner liner. GORUCK warns against gravel because sharp edges damage the bag [6].
- **Scores:** F4 A3 C5.

**4. Water bladder / drinkable weight (starter or top-up only)**
- **What:** A flat 2–4 L bladder in the weight compartment or the backpack sleeve. Pour it out at the office (or water the plants) and refill for the trip home.
- **Requirements:** Max ~4 kg in a sensible volume. Slosh is a real problem; it needs a baffled bladder or full fill. Quick drop: dump the water.
- **Cost:** Nothing to ship. Bladders cost $3–8 (E).
- **Risks:** Leaks next to the laptop. The separate compartment (R-W1) is essential.
- **Scores:** F4 A3 C5. Good as a **"day-one, zero-hassle" starter**, not as a system.

### B. Locally sourced standard items

**5. Steel flat bar, cut to length locally ("buy a bar, not a brick")**
- **What:** Publish a spec, e.g. "100 × 10 mm mild steel flat bar, cut to 380 mm". Any metal stockist cuts this, often for free. One piece = 380 cm³ ≈ **3.0 kg**. Use 100 × 5 mm for **1.5 kg** half-steps. We ship only light **TPU/rubber sleeves with end caps** that silence the bar, protect the bag and carry a weight label.
- **Requirements:** Full steel density (7.85), so **24 kg = 8 bars = 8 cm stack**: the slimmest of all options apart from our own bricks. Exact, repeatable weight. Every bar fits every slot. A sleeve plus tray clamp makes it rattle-free. Steps of 1.5 or 3 kg meet R-W2.
- **Shipping:** Sleeves only, ~50 g each.
- **Cost to customer:** UK list price is ~£17/m ex VAT for 100 × 10 mm [7], so ~£6.50 per 3 kg bar, **~£52 for 24 kg**. Plus local pickup.
- **Model:** Sell a sleeve kit at $25–40, and a partner-stockist directory ("Heavy Bag-ready" metal suppliers).
- **Customer effort:** Medium. A one-time trip to a metal yard is intimidating for some office workers. A stockist that ships locally solves this; regional steel is cheap to ship short distances.
- **Risks:** Rust (oil it, or use the sleeve), sharp cut ends (end caps, or require deburring), and a dropped bar on a foot. US equivalent: 4" × 3/8" flat bar.
- **Scores:** F5 A3 C5. **Sleeper hit**: the only "local" option that matches steel compactness.

**6. Standard steel washers or plate offcuts on a rod or in a sleeve**
- **What:** Large steel washers or fender washers, or laser-cut offcuts, stacked in a capped tube cassette. Stack efficiency is ~80% (E).
- **Assessment:** Works everywhere, but there are many small parts and the weight per unit is fiddly to assemble. Inferior to #5.
- **Scores:** F3 A2 C4.

**7. Real building bricks, per country, in a "brick cosy"**
- **What:** Fabric or TPU sleeves sized to the local standard brick. UK 215 × 102.5 × 65 mm, ~3.1–3.7 kg. US modular ~194 × 92 × 57 mm, ~2 kg. German/NL NF/DF 240 × 115 × 71 mm (often perforated, so lighter) [8].
- **Requirements:** Universal and cheap (< $1 each), with a ~2–3.5 kg step. But bricks are bulky (24 kg ≈ 7 UK bricks ≈ 10 L), dusty and abrasive, and sizes vary by country, which means SKU sprawl. Perforated bricks give inconsistent weight.
- **Shipping:** Sleeves only.
- **Scores:** F4 A2 C5. The founder's existing "Concept E": fine as a budget/backup mode, not core.

**8. Office paper reams, books or laptops**
- **What:** "Carry what you already have."
- **Assessment:** Density < 1.2, so it is bulky and capped at ~5 kg. Fun marketing ("carry your reading list"), useless as a system.
- **Scores:** F3 A2 C5.

**9. Dense floor or porcelain tiles in a stack (backpack sleeve)**
- **What:** Porcelain tiles (~2.3–2.4 g/cm³, E) cut to sleeve size, e.g. 28 × 38 cm × 10 mm ≈ 2.5 kg each, and sold everywhere.
- **Assessment:** 24 kg would be ~10 cm thick, so ~10 kg max. Tiles are brittle; they crack, leave sharp shards and rattle.
- **Scores:** F2 A1 C5. Mostly a reject; listed for completeness.

### C. Compatibility with existing fitness gear

**10. Standard 1" (25 mm) plate adapter: "toast-rack" tray**
- **What:** The tray has a 25 mm peg/rod and holds standard-hole cast-iron plates **on edge**. A 2.5 kg standard plate is ~14 cm diameter × 25 mm thick [9]. Ten plates = 25 kg in a 25 cm × 14 cm × 14 cm block, under the handle in the briefcase base. A spin-lock collar clamps them, so no rattle. 1.25 kg plates give fine steps.
- **Requirements:** Effective density with round-in-square packing is ~5.6 g/cm³. A 14 cm-tall band is taller than a flat 7–9 cm steel tray, but the centre of mass stays low. Quick drop: lift out the rod and plates as one unit, which doubles as a mini dumbbell handle (nice!). Diameters vary by brand (14–20 cm [9]), so specify **"plates ≤ 15 cm diameter"**, or offer two rack sizes.
- **Shipping and cost:** Ship the rack and collar only. Customers already own plates, or buy them used at ~$0.5–1/lb (E) or new locally.
- **Model:** Sell the rack as an accessory at $30–50 (E).
- **Customer effort:** Low for anyone who owns dumbbell plates. Many home gyms have exactly these.
- **Risks:** Bare iron rusts and chips (use rubber-coated plates or a sleeve). Plates with odd diameters won't fit.
- **Scores:** F4 A4 C5. **Strong; on-brand for the secondary persona.**

**11. Olympic (50 mm) fractional and change plates**
- **What:** 2.5 kg Olympic plates are ~21 cm diameter (E); 1.25 kg are ~16 cm.
- **Assessment:** Too tall for the briefcase base. Fits the backpack back-panel sleeve lying flat. Change plates (0.5–2.5 kg) are good for micro-progression.
- **Scores:** F3 A3 C5. Fold this into #12's backpack sleeve geometry.

**12. GORUCK-compatible ruck-plate sleeve (backpack)**
- **What:** Size the backpack sleeve to the de-facto standard ruck plate: GORUCK "standard" is **9 × 11.5 in (22.9 × 29.2 cm)**, 20 lb (9 kg) at 0.92 in and 30 lb (13.6 kg) at 1.38 in thick. "Long" plates are 9.5 × 15 in [10]. 20 lb + 30 lb ≈ 22.7 kg in ~5.8 cm.
- **Requirements:** Dense (cast iron), slab-shaped, ideal for a back-panel sleeve. Big steps (4.5 kg) unless combined with smaller items. Third-party clones exist widely.
- **Shipping and cost:** We ship nothing; the customer buys from GORUCK, Rogue or Amazon clones, or already owns them.
- **Appeal:** The rucking community is a ready-made audience, and the backpack becomes a "work-legal ruck".
- **Scores:** F5 A4 C5. **Must-do for the backpack** (near-zero cost: just pick the right sleeve dimensions).

**13. Sandbag-insert / weight-vest-plate compatibility**
- **What:** Accept common weight-vest plates (e.g. small 1–2.5 kg steel slabs used in adjustable vests) or GORUCK sandbag filler bags.
- **Assessment:** Fragmented standards, but a flexible clamp tray (see hybrid) would take many of them.
- **Scores:** F3 A3 C5.

**14. Kettlebell or dumbbell "docking"**
- **What:** The bag base accepts a compact dumbbell head.
- **Assessment:** Irregular shapes, lumpy and hard to centre. Reject for the briefcase.
- **Scores:** F2 A2 C5.

### D. Supply chain and distribution models

**15. Regional manufacturing and 3PL of our own bricks**
- **What:** Cast or cut coated steel bricks in or near each market (EU foundries in PL/CZ/PT, a US foundry, UK) or import by ocean freight. Ship from a regional 3PL using bulk-negotiated parcel rates.
- **Cost:** ~$25–35 per 24 kg shipped at volume (E). Bricks cost ~$2–4/kg (E).
- **Assessment:** Best experience: exact, quiet, branded and slim. Inventory and working capital are heavy.
- **Scores:** F4 A5 C2.

**16. "Progression subscription": ship increments, not sets**
- **What:** Bag plus a starter of 2 × 2.5 kg (or a fill kit). Then 2.5 kg every 2–4 weeks as the training plan advances: a "level-up" box, ~5 kg per parcel.
- **Requirements:** Matches the training curve. Small parcels avoid oversize/overweight surcharges. Total shipping per kg is somewhat higher, but it's spread out, often paid by the customer, and **each box is a retention touch** (plus a coaching email and a progress badge). Customers who plateau at 12 kg never need the other 12 kg shipped.
- **Model:** $X/month, or pay-per-level, cancellable.
- **Scores:** F4 A4 C3. **Great business-model wrapper for whatever dense brick we sell.**

**17. Retail / click-and-collect partners**
- **What:** Sell bricks (or approved filler, flat bar or plates) through fitness chains, hardware chains, outdoor stores or Decathlon-type stores, which already move heavy freight by pallet. The customer picks them up in-store.
- **Assessment:** Pallet freight per kg is tiny, but retail margin takes 40–50%. Needs scale before partners care.
- **Scores:** F3 A4 C4.

**18. Licensing the brick spec to fitness-equipment makers**
- **What:** Publish an open "HB-Brick" standard (footprint, thickness steps, edge radius, coating). Let plate makers and Amazon sellers produce compatible bricks. We sell bags and trays; they sell metal, with their own freight economics.
- **Assessment:** Turns the weights into an ecosystem like GORUCK plates. Low capex. Risks losing quality control; mitigate with a "Certified" mark.
- **Scores:** F3 A3 C5 (long-term play).

**19. Group-buy pallet drops to community hubs**
- **What:** Ship a pallet of bricks to a gym, run club, coworking space or office. Members collect locally. LTL pallet freight costs maybe $0.2–0.5/kg (E) versus $1–3/kg parcel.
- **Scores:** F3 A3 C4. Good for launch cities and pre-order campaigns.

**20. B2B / corporate-wellness office sets**
- **What:** Employer buys a "weight station" (rack + 100–200 kg of bricks) for the office, shipped by pallet. Employees carry weights home and back, or keep a home set and an office set. Each employee's personal weight is labelled.
- **Assessment:** Solves quick-drop (UC-2) elegantly. One pallet ships cheaply. Wellness budgets pay. Liability and insurance need review.
- **Scores:** F3 A4 C4.

**21. Rental / swap / buy-back pool**
- **What:** Bricks are leased. When you level up, swap two 2.5 kg bricks for one 5 kg brick, or return them when done. A refurb loop recoats and re-ships.
- **Assessment:** Reverse logistics of metal is as expensive as forward logistics, so swaps only work locally (retail partner or hub). Buy-back credit is nice for brand loyalty.
- **Scores:** F2 A3 C2.

**22. Peer marketplace for second-hand weights**
- **What:** In-app or community page (or just a Facebook-group guide). Used plates and bricks change hands locally at ~$0.5–1/lb (E).
- **Assessment:** Pairs with #10 and #12 (standard plates are abundant second-hand).
- **Scores:** F4 A2 C5 (cheap to run as a guide; low priority as a product).

### E. Unconventional

**23. Dense granular "ballast pack" bought locally from industrial suppliers**
- **What:** We certify a short list of locally available dense fillers by region: steel shot or grit (blast media; India ~₹50–100/kg, China under $1/kg bulk, US retail ~$4/kg for a 50 lb bag [11]), and barite or magnetite sand where available. These go into cassettes (#2).
- **Assessment:** The most "DIY at steel-ish density" option: 24 kg in ~5.5 L.
- **Effort:** Finding blast media locally takes effort: welding and sandblast suppliers, or Amazon/eBay. The latter ships it to the customer anyway, but the cost then falls on the customer/seller and is untied from our margin.
- **Safety:** Steel shot is non-toxic, but **lead shot must be explicitly banned** (R-K5).
- **Scores:** F4 A3 C4.

**24. "Weight-in-the-frame" option: a removable steel bar as the frame member**
- **What:** The bag ships with a light aluminium base bar. Users can swap it for a heavier steel bar (bought locally per #5), so the frame doubles as weight.
- **Assessment:** Elegant, but it conflates the structure with the drop mechanism, and a user could remove a structural part. Probably only as a 2–3 kg "base weight" built in.
- **Scores:** F2 A3 C4.

**25. Print-on-demand shells + local fill**
- **What:** We publish STL files for the tray, cassette and adapters. Users or makerspaces print them (PETG/ASA), then fill them with shot or epoxy-plus-shot.
- **Assessment:** Zero shipping, very on-trend with hobbyists, but print quality is uneven and load-bearing reliability is unproven. Good for adapters, not for sealed containers in a 30 kg system.
- **Scores:** F2 A2 C5.

## 2. Ranked shortlist

1. **Universal cassette with fill-your-own option (#2, with #23 fillers).** Ships at ~2 kg. Steel shot gives ~5 kg per cassette, so 24 kg fits in ~5.5 L. Sand or water gives a cheap starter. Every cassette is identical, so R-K1 holds and the tray design stays simple.
2. **Own premium coated steel bricks, sold as a progression subscription (#15 + #16), not as one 24 kg box.** Keeps the best-in-class experience (slim, silent, marked, exact). Shipping happens in small, monetised increments only when users need them.
3. **Local steel flat bar + sleeve kit (#5).** Steel density with no metal shipped. Exact weights. Perfect geometric fit with a 38 cm tray. The cheapest path to 24 kg in a slim briefcase.
4. **Gym-gear adapters: 1" plate toast-rack (#10) for the briefcase, ruck-plate-sized sleeve (#12) for the backpack.** Uses gear people already own. Near-zero cost to us. Strong appeal to the fitness-minded persona.
5. _(Honourable mention)_ **B2B office sets and pallet hubs (#19, #20).** The best freight economics, and they solve the "leave it under the desk" use case directly. Phase 2.

The mould kit (#1) ranked lower than the founder might expect. Concrete is too low-density for anything beyond ~10 kg in a slim bag, and the effort, dust and crumbling make it a poor first experience. Keep it as a hobbyist or budget accessory, ideally in the shot-loaded variant (#1b).

## 3. Recommended hybrid strategy: "One tray, any weight"

**Principle:** The bag sells a **weight interface**, not weights. Define one standard, the **HB Cassette Standard**, and make every weight source conform to it.

### 3.1 The standard (proposed; to be validated by prototype)
- **Slot module:** e.g. **380 × 100 mm footprint**, with heights in steps: 10 mm ("half"), 20 mm, 35 mm. For the backpack, a second footprint matches the ruck plate (**229 × 292 mm**, up to ~60 mm total stack).
- **Tray:** A rigid aluminium/HDPE tray with an end handle (R-W9) that slides into the drawer compartment (Concept A).
  - A **compression lid or cam strap** clamps any stack from 10 to 90 mm tall, which removes rattle regardless of source (R-W7).
  - A two-step latch (R-W6).
  - The tray limits total volume, so even all-steel can't exceed ~24 kg (R-W10).
- **Everything goes in via the tray:**

  | Source | How it fits | Typical increment |
  |---|---|---|
  | Our steel bricks (optional, premium) | Native module, TPU-coated, laser-marked | 1.25 / 2.5 / 5 kg |
  | Fill cassettes (shipped with the bag) | Native module; fill with shot, sand or water | ~1–5 kg |
  | Flat-bar sleeves | 100 × 10 or 100 × 5 mm bar cut to 380 mm, in a TPU sleeve | 3 / 1.5 kg |
  | 1" plate toast-rack insert | Replaces the tray's flat floor (accessory) | 1.25 / 2.5 kg |
  | Ruck plates (backpack) | Sleeve sized to the GORUCK standard, with a strap clamp | 4.5 / 9 / 13.6 kg |
  | Bricks in cosies / water bladder | "Budget mode": open tray with strap, lower max | 1–3.5 kg |

- **Knowing the weight (Q-10):** Variable sources (fill, bricks, plates) need weighing, so **include a small hanging luggage scale** (cost ~$3–5, E). The tray's handle hooks straight onto it. One habit, "hang the tray, read the number", closes Q-10 for every source.

### 3.2 Commercial packaging
- **Bag box (~3–4 kg):** bag, tray, 4–6 empty fill cassettes, 2 flat-bar sleeves, scale, and a 6-week progression card. One parcel, normal shipping.
- **Optional Starter Steel:** 2 × 2.5 kg bricks, either in the bag box (still ≤ 10 kg) or as a separate box. Gives a premium out-of-box experience without a 24 kg shipment.
- **Level-up subscription or one-off packs (5 kg per box):** for customers who want our steel, timed to the training plan.
- **Accessory kits:** plate toast-rack, flat-bar sleeve 4-pack, extra cassettes, shot funnel.
- **Phase 2:** retail partners and office/hub pallets for our steel bricks; publish the open HB spec so third parties can make compatible weights.

### 3.3 Why this wins
- Shipping per customer drops from ~27 kg to **~3–8 kg** in the default path.
- Every price tier and persona is served: DIY or budget, fitness-gear owners, premium.
- Engineering stays sane: **one** clamp tray handles rattle, release and overload, whatever the contents.
- Brand stays premium: our steel bricks are the "best" option, not the only one.

### 3.4 Design implications for the repo docs (suggestions for the parent)
- Q-1 is reframed: "own bricks **and** a compatible standard".
- Add a requirement for the clamp range and the ruck-plate sleeve dimensions.
- Concept A (drawer) plus a compression clamp becomes the favourite.
- Concept E (bring-your-own) merges into the tray.
- Size the briefcase base for the tallest planned option. Plates on edge need a **~14–15 cm** band, versus ~8–9 cm for flat steel and ~14 cm for 24 kg of shot. **Decide whether the briefcase supports 24 kg of shot or caps it at ~15 kg** (a fork for the design team).

## 4. Key experiments to validate

| # | Experiment | Method | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Real shipping quotes** | Quote 3PL/parcel rates (US, UK, DE, NL) for 5 kg, 12 kg and 24 kg boxes, domestic and cross-border; compare with pallet LTL to a hub. | Know the true $/kg; confirm the ≤12–15 kg split threshold. |
| E2 | **Clamp-tray rattle test** | Prototype one tray. Load each source (steel bricks, shot cassettes, sand cassettes, flat bar in sleeves, 1" plates on a rod, UK brick in a cosy). Walk 20 min with a phone accelerometer and a mic, then the drop-on-desk test. | No audible rattle; drop under 10 s for every source. |
| E3 | **Cassette and pouch durability** | Filled cassettes and pouches with steel shot: 10,000 lift cycles, 1 m drops, cap-torque loss, and a leak check with fine sand. | Zero leaks; caps hold. |
| E4 | **Local-sourcing mystery shop** | 5 testers in 3–4 cities source (a) 380 mm flat bar, (b) steel shot, (c) 1" plates ≤ 15 cm, (d) all-purpose sand. Time and cost each. | Each available within 30 min travel / 1 order at under ~$4/kg; note friction. |
| E5 | **Customer preference test** | Landing-page or pre-order A/B test of the weight options (included steel at +$X, progression subscription, DIY kit only), plus a 10-person interview set split by persona. | Conversion and price sensitivity by option; whether DIY lowers or raises perceived value. |
| E6 | **Progression reality check** | Wear testers log their added weight weekly for 8 weeks. | Distribution of weight actually used (does anyone reach 24 kg? when?). Sets subscription cadence and how much steel to include. |
| E7 | **Briefcase geometry** | Mock-ups with a 24 kg shot cassette stack (~14 cm), a plate rack (~14 cm) and a flat-steel stack (~8 cm). Check looks, leg clearance and centre of mass. | Pick the max height for the base band; decide a shot cap for the briefcase. |
| E8 | **Concrete casting sanity test** (low priority) | Cast 5 bricks of normal concrete and 5 with steel-shot aggregate in a prototype mould. Measure density, chipping and dust after 2 weeks of carry. | Confirms or kills the mould kit. |
| E9 | **Weighing UX (Q-10)** | Testers use the luggage-scale-on-tray-handle flow. | Under 15 s, and judged accurate to within ±0.5 kg. |

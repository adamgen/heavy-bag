# Brand Direction (interim)

Status: **working direction**, drafted while the founder is focused
elsewhere. Founder to review. Prices are hypotheses to test in the
competitor scan and landing-page test.

## Positioning

**A strength tool disguised as a work bag.**

Premium, professional, built like equipment. It belongs in a boardroom and
quietly does the work of a gym. We don't sell a gadget or a gimmick; we sell
a habit — strength built a little every day, with no extra time.

- **For:** office workers who walk to work, care about their health, and
  have no time for it.
- **Against:** regular work bags (look good, do nothing), rucking packs (look
  like gym or military gear), gym memberships (cost time).
- **Proof:** a 30 kg-rated aluminium frame, metal hardware, and fabric that
  passes the same tests whatever the finish.

## Personality

Quietly strong. Disciplined. Understated, with a dry sense of humour. Sure
of itself without shouting. Think well-made tool, not fitness influencer.

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Calm, confident, precise | Loud, hype-driven |
| Built and engineered | Flimsy, trendy |
| Office-ready | Gym gear, tactical gear |
| Encouraging, practical | Preachy, "no pain no gain" |

## Look

- **Silhouette:** clean and structured — the frame gives crisp, straight
  lines that read as premium. No bulging pockets, no exposed MOLLE webbing.
- **Colours:** matte black, charcoal, deep olive, sand. Tonal (same-colour)
  hardware and zips.
- **Signature detail:** the end of the weight cassette is visible as a
  clean metal or moulded cap, with a small engraved load marker. It's the
  only visible hint of what the bag does — a talking point, not a logo.
- **Logo and type:** small, tonal, debossed; simple sans-serif type.
- **Photography:** real commutes — city streets, stairs, office lobbies,
  early light. People mid-stride carrying the bag at their side. No gym
  shots, no flexing.

## Marketing notes

- **Add-on scale.** A small add-on scale (hang the bag from it, read the
  total) so customers hit their target load — e.g. "you're at 24 kg, add one
  more weight". Good accessory, bundle item and content hook. Not a core
  product feature. The weights research suggests bundling a cheap one that
  hooks onto the tray handle, since self-filled and local weights vary.
- **Progress story.** Content and community built around building up to
  30 kg over weeks: "kg carried this month", commute challenges, before /
  after grip-strength tests.
- **Expert credibility.** Carry guide co-written with a strength coach or
  physiotherapist.
- **The reveal.** The bag looks normal until someone picks it up — great for
  short videos ("lift my bag").

## Name

**Heavy Bag** — the founder's choice: simple, says what it is, natural to
the audience.

The desk check ([`research/name-check.md`](research/name-check.md)) found
the old "HEAVY BAG" trademark for bags appears cancelled (2023), so there's
no obvious blocker, but flagged three things to manage:

- **Weak protection.** The words describe the product, so register a
  distinctive **logo / stylised mark**, not only the words, and build
  recognition through the look (the visible weight cassette, the colours).
- **Boxing search results.** "Heavy bag" searches return punching bags.
  Lead search and ads with "weighted work bag", "commute workout bag",
  "suitcase carry bag"; let the brand name ride on top.
- **Domain.** heavybag.com is probably taken (unverified). Fallbacks in
  order: buy it if affordable, then theheavybag.com, heavybag.co,
  getheavybag.com, heavybagco.com.

**Product names that fit:** the **Heavy Pack** (backpack) and the
**Heavy Brief** (laptop bag).

**If "Heavy Bag" is blocked,** stay close to it: Heavy Bag Co., The Heavy
Bag, Heavybag (one word), Heavy Carry, Carry Heavy, Heavy Pack. See the name
check for details.

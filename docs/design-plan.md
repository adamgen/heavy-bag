# Product design and POC plan

Current work is **product design only**. Audience: office workers who lift
heavy. Start light; 30 kg is the maximum complete bag mass, not a required
progression goal. [Requirements](product-requirements.md) and
[tech pack](tech-pack.md) replace the earlier v0.1 sizing and capacity claims.

## 1. Sequence and gates

| Step | Output | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Packaging revision | Coherent provisional dimensions, SVGs, source capacities | Finished parts, laptop and hardware can coexist on paper and in fit gauges |
| 2. Design comparisons | Mock-ups of extraction, handle modules and backpack placement | Choose mechanisms based on controlled handling evidence |
| 3. POC preparation | Parts/sample list for Israel or abroad, reviewed frame and fasteners | Product choices settled; no orders authorized by this plan |
| 4. Product POC | Framed samples, surrogate-laptop tests, progressively loaded rig tests | A product the founder likes, with recorded failures resolved |
| Later | Wear-test refinement; ads or Kickstarter; launch/manufacturing planning | After satisfying product POC |

Demand interviews, ads, legal/trademark review, shipping studies and sales
channels are **not gates for present product design**. US fitness-brand steel
shipping is accepted as a workable precedent; this revision does not reopen
that business premise. This defers legal work, not mechanical safety testing.

## 2. Provisional packaging, not engineering approval

Full arithmetic and source-specific limits: [tech pack §§2–4](tech-pack.md).

| Enclosure outer, mm | 406 L × 114 D × 106 H | 307 W × 322 H × 66 D |

| Clear bay, mm | 414 L × 122 D × 110 H | 319 W × 334 H × 78 D |
| Clear laptop sleeve W × H × D, mm | 370 × 260 × 25 | 260 × 370 × 25, portrait |

Nominal 95 × 100 steel cores become finished parts ≤97 × 102 ×35 (full) or
×18 (half). Edge and intermodule gaps are 2 mm. Four finished widths plus gaps
need 398 mm, not 380. Three need 299, not 285. Big Pack needs 322 enclosure height,
not the previous 300 mm zone; the enclosing bay is 334. Side/frame allowance
raises the body to 360 wide, not an unsupported 330 mm fix.

These sizes trade compactness for real assembly space. Validate leg/stair
clearance of the taller laptop bag, portrait laptop access in Big Pack,
backpack thickness, and remaining daily-contents volume. No existing “8 L”
claim is retained. Mock-ups can reject this envelope before any order.

## 3. Weight interface and capacities

Keep the shared weight interface, not a literal interchangeable tray between
bags. 4 × 1 laptop tray vs 3 × 3 Big Pack carrier; adapters and source geometry
differ. The finished mass must be measured: a 95 ×100 ×33 steel block is about
2.461 kg before rounding/coating, not exactly 2.5 kg. Tune nominal 2.5 /1.25 kg
bricks within finished envelopes; agree mass tolerances before fabrication.

The illustrative 24 kg allowance assumes 2.5 empty +2 laptop +1.5 contents.
Four 6.25 kg stacks =25, so are not allowed in that example. Eight full +three
half =23.75 kg in the laptop tray; nine full +one half =23.75 in Big Pack.
Every loaded bag must be weighed; volume cannot enforce the 30 kg ceiling.

No “full 24 kg from every source”: seven sleeved 380 mm bars fit the laptop
stack (~20.88 kg steel); eight do not. Big Pack holds two rows of sleeved 100 mm-wide bar, not three after gaps
and edges; eight pieces give ~17.90 kg steel. Secure the unused space.
Shot shells lose volume to walls/closures; sand/water lower still. Plates
require actual SKU checks, padded thickness limits and compatible clamps.

## 4. Extraction comparison — open proposal

Keep the bag set down. Compare:

- **A: supported single-cassette slide-out.** Adjacent stable support, two
  stowable lifting grips, positive travel stop, deliberate final release after
  support. Confirm hatch clear 118 ×110 after zip/lining intrusion, handle
  clearance and continuous cradle load path around the hatch. Do not transfer
  a heavy cassette by one unsupported wrist. Laptop tray support length starts
  at 450 mm for comparison; determine capacity, stability and pinch protection.
- **B: two smaller cassettes.** Lower individual mass but extra walls/clamps/
  releases; loss of full-length bar compatibility. Split geometry not frozen.

Compare removal time, wrist effort, need for support, reset/lock indication,
rattle and partial-load stability. The old one-motion / under 10 s requirement
is withdrawn pending evidence. Big Pack's top-lift also needs two-grip handling
and rig screening; do not assume a single pull loop is adequate at heavy load.

## 5. Laptop isolation — approved design addition

Suspend the sleeve from reinforced upper structure, clear of rigid weight
barriers. Laptop bag base stack:10 cradle +106 cassette +4 clearance +4
barrier +6 replaceable pad +12 free gap +272 padded sleeve +16 top =430 mm.
This is unloaded packaging, not proof of shock attenuation. Big Pack has a
rigid separator,12 mm lateral gap (6 pads +6 free), and 12 free vertical gap
above a 6 pad on a 14 mm base stop. Portrait padded sleeve is 272 ×382 ×37.

Use a laptop surrogate for base/corner drops. Measure suspension stretch,
residual gap, hard contact and transmitted shock; define an acceptable shock
limit before testing. Pads are service parts. A rigid HDPE divider alone is
not laptop protection, and the frame must not bypass the isolation.

## 6. Modular handles — approved direction, details open

Frame-mounted common receivers at provisional 140 mm centres take:
1. A fabric grip module with structural end fittings.
2. A standard rigid weight-handle module;120 usable grip length, ≥45 clear
   knuckle space, compare 30/35/40 mm grips.
3. A requested **double weight handle**, explicitly **TBD**. Clarify meaning
   before selecting geometry; no assumed two-grip or two-hand design.

POC swaps use captive bolts, unloaded, with a tool. Receiver/bolt diameter,
engagement, anti-loosening, torque and edge distances require review. No
quick-release, sewn-only handle anchors or universal commercial-handle-fit
claim. Each complete module needs the same structural testing. Position over
the actual loaded centre of mass and compare left/right handling at light and
heavy test configurations; no guessed fixed CoM in the drawing.

## 7. Backpack placement comparison — open proposal

Both layouts put the carrier close to the back frame. Compare separate fixed
low (bottom 36 above base) and mid-low (bottom 66) carriers: same 322 height,
same mass,30 mm offset. Bays 334 high fit either layout in the 500 body.
No adjustable slider in the POC. Compare hand-carry lean and swing, shoulder
pressure when worn, strap stow, laptop access, and main-compartment intrusion.
Low is not automatically better for the worn mode. Rucking remains secondary.

## 8. Construction and test loop

6061-T6 spine and HDPE panels remain the structural direction. Core nylon,
reinforced seams, metal load-path fittings, backup load webbing and replaceable
runners/pads/feet. ≤2.5 kg empty is a stretch hypothesis, not a constraint to
remove required safety structure. Record the mass of each handle configuration.

1. Build unloaded fit gauges including coatings, clamp hardware, folded grips,
   zipper intrusions and frame/textile allowance. Cardboard is for fit only.
2. Make structurally reviewed mock-ups before adding significant mass. Start
   light; do not have a person carry 30 kg in a cardboard/duct-tape mock-up.
3. Screen retention and handling on controlled rigs, then comfortable walks.
4. Engineer-review static proof (proposed 90 kg equivalent),30 kg fatigue/creep,
   backup containment and enclosure wear tests. These are targets, not results.
5. Drop-test surrogate laptop and inspect isolation, frame, lid and retention.
6. Resolve A/B extraction and low/mid-low position; revise dimensions and SVGs.
7. Prepare sample/parts order after choices settle; founder reviews before buy.
